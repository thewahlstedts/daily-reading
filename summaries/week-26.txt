In chapter 31 the people tear down pillars, Asherah poles and high places, and their firstfruits and tithes for the priests and Levites pile up in heaps, because God has blessed his people. Whatever Hezekiah undertakes to seek his God, he does with all his heart, and he prospers. In chapter 32 Sennacherib king of Assyria invades. Hezekiah stops up the springs outside the city, rebuilds the walls, and tells the people that with the king of Assyria is only an arm of flesh, but with them is the LORD their God to fight their battles. Sennacherib's servants mock, saying no god has saved any nation from Assyria. Hezekiah and the prophet Isaiah pray, God sends an angel to destroy the Assyrian commanders, and Sennacherib goes home in shame and is killed in the house of his god. Later Hezekiah falls ill and prays, grows proud, and then humbles himself.

His son Manasseh in chapter 33 undoes it all: he rebuilds the high places, worships the host of heaven, burns his sons in the fire, practices sorcery, and sets an idol in God's house. When he will not listen, Assyrian commanders take him to Babylon with hooks and bronze fetters. There, in distress, he humbles himself greatly and prays, and God hears him and brings him back to Jerusalem, where he knows that the LORD is God. He removes the foreign gods and restores the altar, though his son Amon follows his worst ways and is murdered. In chapter 34 Josiah begins to seek God while still a boy and purges the land of idols. While the temple is being repaired, Hilkiah the priest finds a scroll of the law of the LORD given through Moses. Josiah tears his clothes on hearing it, and the prophetess Huldah says disaster will come on Jerusalem for abandoning God, but because Josiah humbled himself and wept, he will be gathered to his grave in peace and not see it. Josiah leads the people in a covenant to keep the words of the scroll with all their heart.

In chapter 35 Josiah keeps a Passover unlike any since the days of Samuel the prophet. Then Neco king of Egypt warns him not to oppose God, who is with Neco, but Josiah disguises himself, fights at Megiddo, is shot by archers and dies, and Jeremiah laments for him. Chapter 36 moves fast: Egypt deposes his son Jehoahaz, the next three kings do evil, and Zedekiah will not humble himself before Jeremiah's word. God had sent messengers again and again out of compassion for his people and his dwelling, but they mocked them until there was no remedy. The king of the Chaldeans kills, plunders, burns the house of God and breaks down Jerusalem's walls, and the survivors go into exile until the land has its Sabbath rest, seventy years, as Jeremiah said. The book ends with Cyrus king of Persia, stirred by God, proclaiming that God has appointed him to build a house in Jerusalem and inviting God's people to go up, the same decree that opens Ezra (Ezra 1:1-3).

Psalm 102 is the prayer of one afflicted, whose days vanish like smoke, who eats ashes like bread and withers like grass, but who trusts that the LORD, who endures forever, will have pity on Zion, rebuild it and hear the prayer of the destitute and the groaning of the prisoner. Placed after the temple burns and the people go into exile, it reads almost like their prayer, though that pairing is an interpretation.
