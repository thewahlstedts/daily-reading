The plan opens with David on the run from Saul. In chapter 23, David asks God whether to rescue Keilah from Philistine raiders, asks again when his men are afraid, and saves the town. When Saul prepares to besiege him there, David asks God through the ephod the priest Abiathar has brought, learns that the town's leaders would hand him over, and leaves with about six hundred men. Jonathan finds him at Horesh, strengthens him through God, and tells him he will be king with Jonathan second to him; the two make a covenant before God. The Ziphites betray David's hiding place, and Saul is closing in around a mountain in the wilderness of Maon when news of a Philistine raid calls him away.

In chapter 24, Saul goes into a cave near En Gedi to relieve himself, not knowing David and his men are deep inside. David's men say God has handed his enemy to him, but David only cuts off the hem of Saul's robe, and even that troubles his conscience, because Saul is God's anointed. Outside, David shows Saul the hem and leaves judgment to God. Saul weeps, says David is more righteous than he, admits David will be king, and has him swear not to wipe out his descendants. Chapter 25 records Samuel's death, then the story of Nabal, a rich, stubborn man who insults David's messengers even though David's men had protected his shepherds. David sets out with four hundred armed men, vowing to leave no male of Nabal's household alive. Nabal's wife Abigail, wise and beautiful, rides out with food, bows before David, and urges him not to carry blood guilt into the leadership God has promised him. David blesses God for sending her and turns back. About ten days later God strikes Nabal and he dies, and David takes Abigail as his wife.

In chapter 26 the Ziphites inform on David again. He and Abishai slip into Saul's camp at night while a deep sleep from God lies on everyone. Abishai offers to pin Saul to the ground with one thrust of the spear; David refuses, saying God will strike him or his day will come, and takes only the spear and the water jar. From a distant hilltop he rebukes Abner for failing to guard the king, and Saul again confesses that he has sinned and acted like a fool.

In chapter 27, David decides Saul will kill him one day and goes over to Achish king of Gath, who gives him Ziklag. During his year and four months there, he raids other peoples, leaves no one alive to report it, and lets Achish believe he has been raiding Judah. Chapter 28 turns back to Saul. With the Philistines camped at Shunem, he is terrified, and God does not answer him by dreams, Urim or prophets. Though he had expelled the mediums from the land, he disguises himself and visits one at Endor, who brings up Samuel. Samuel says God has torn the kingdom from him and given it to David because he disobeyed over Amalek, and that tomorrow Saul and his sons will be with him. Saul collapses, and the woman feeds him before he leaves in the night. Psalm 70, a short psalm of David, asks God to hurry to help and to shame those who seek the psalmist's life; after a week of pursuit it reads like a hunted man's prayer, though that pairing is an interpretation.
