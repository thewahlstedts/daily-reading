The plan turns to Luke this week. Chapter 1 opens with a note to Theophilus: this account is set down in order so that he may know the certainty of what he was taught. The angel Gabriel promises the elderly priest Zechariah that his barren wife Elizabeth will bear a son, John, who will go before the Lord in the spirit and power of Elijah; Zechariah doubts and is left unable to speak. Six months later Gabriel tells Mary, a virgin in Nazareth promised in marriage to Joseph, that she will bear a son, Jesus, who will be called the Son of the Most High and reign on David's throne forever, and she answers that she is the Lord's servant. Visiting Elizabeth, Mary praises God, who brings down rulers and lifts up the lowly. When John is circumcised on the eighth day, Zechariah writes "John is his name," his speech returns, and he praises God for remembering his covenant with Abraham.

In chapter 2, a decree of Caesar Augustus brings Joseph and Mary to Bethlehem, where Jesus is born and laid in a manger. An angel announces good news of great joy to shepherds, a heavenly army praises God, and the shepherds go and find the baby. At the temple, Simeon takes the child in his arms and calls him a light for the Gentiles and glory for Israel, and the aged prophetess Anna gives thanks. At twelve, Jesus stays behind in Jerusalem, and his parents find him among the teachers in the temple, and he asks them why they did not know he must be in his Father's house. In chapter 3, John preaches a baptism of repentance, tells the crowds, tax collectors and soldiers to share and deal fairly, and points to one more powerful who will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire; Herod later locks John in prison. At Jesus' baptism the Spirit descends like a dove and a voice from heaven calls him the beloved Son. His genealogy then runs back through Zerubbabel, David, Boaz and Abraham to Adam, names readers of 1 Chronicles will recognize.

In chapter 4, Jesus answers each of the devil's temptations in the wilderness from Scripture. In the synagogue at Nazareth he reads from Isaiah about good news for the poor and release for captives and says it is fulfilled today. When he reminds the town that Elijah was sent to a widow in Sidon and that Elisha cleansed only Naaman the Syrian (2 Kings 5, read in week 10), they try to throw him off a cliff. In Capernaum he drives out an unclean spirit, heals Simon's mother-in-law and many others, and says he must proclaim the kingdom of God in other towns too. In chapter 5, after a night of catching nothing, Simon lets down his nets at Jesus' word, the catch nearly sinks two boats, and Simon, James and John leave everything to follow him. Jesus cleanses a man with leprosy, forgives and heals a paralyzed man lowered through a roof, and calls the tax collector Levi, saying he came to call sinners to repentance. In chapter 6, he defends his disciples for picking grain on the Sabbath, heals a withered hand, and after a night of prayer chooses twelve apostles. Then he blesses the poor, the hungry and those who weep, warns the rich, and teaches love for enemies, mercy, and building on rock by doing what he says.

Psalm 87 celebrates Zion and names those who know God in Rahab, Babylon, Philistia, Tyre and Cush, recorded with the words "This one was born there." Psalm 88, a maskil of Heman the Ezrahite, is a lament that cries to God day and night and ends in darkness. One way to read the pairing is that Psalm 87's nations echo Simeon's light for the Gentiles, though that link is an interpretation.
