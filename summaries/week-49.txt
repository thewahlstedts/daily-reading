Malachi 4 ends the book. A day is coming, burning like an oven, when the arrogant and the evildoers will be stubble, but for those who revere God's name the sun of righteousness will rise with healing in its wings, and they will leap like fattened calves. God tells them to remember the instruction of Moses and promises to send Elijah the prophet before the great and awesome day of the LORD, to turn the hearts of fathers to their sons and sons to their fathers. In Matthew 11:14 Jesus says of John the Baptist that, if they are willing to accept it, he is the Elijah who is to come.

Then the plan turns to Hosea, whose word from God came in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash, king of Israel, and of four kings of Judah. In chapter 1 God tells him to take a wife of whoredom, because the land has forsaken the LORD, and he marries Gomer. Their children's names carry messages: Jezreel, because God will punish the house of Jehu for the blood shed at Jezreel; Lo-ruhamah, "no pity," because he will no longer have pity on Israel; and Lo-ammi, "not my people." Yet the chapter ends with a reversal: Israel will be as many as the sand of the sea, and where they were told "You are not my people," they will be called children of the living God. Chapter 2 pictures Israel as an unfaithful wife who chased lovers and credited them, rather than God, with her grain, wine and oil, and made the gold he gave into a Baal. God will end her festivals and strip her, but then draw her into the wilderness, speak tenderly to her, and make the Valley of Achor a door of hope. She will call him "my husband," not "my Baal," and he will take her as his wife forever in righteousness, justice, steadfast love, mercy and faithfulness. In chapter 3 God tells Hosea to love a woman who is committing adultery, as God loves Israel though they turn to other gods; Hosea buys her and tells her to remain his for many days, as Israel will long be without king or sacrifice before returning to seek God and David their king.

Chapter 4 brings God's indictment of the land. There is no faithfulness, no loyalty and no knowledge of God; swearing, lying, murder, theft and adultery break out, and the land mourns. The priests come in for blame: God's people are destroyed for lack of knowledge, and it will be "like people, like priest." In chapter 5 priests, people and king all fall under judgment. When Ephraim saw his sickness he went to Assyria for a cure, but God himself will be like a lion to Ephraim and Judah, and he will withdraw until they seek his face.

Chapter 6 opens with a call to return to the LORD, who has torn and will heal, and will raise his people up on the third day. Then God says their love is like a morning cloud, like dew that disappears early, and that he desires steadfast love, not sacrifice, and knowledge of God more than burnt offerings, a line Jesus cites, as mercy and not sacrifice, in Matthew 9:13 and 12:7. Chapter 7 pictures Ephraim as a cake not turned over and a senseless dove, calling to Egypt and going to Assyria. In chapter 8 Israel has made kings without God and a calf in Samaria that is no god; they sow the wind and will reap the whirlwind, and Israel has forgotten its maker.
