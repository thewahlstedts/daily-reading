In Luke 18 Jesus tells of a widow who wears down an unjust judge, as a lesson to keep praying, and of a Pharisee and a tax collector at prayer: the one who begs for mercy goes home justified. He tells a rich ruler to sell everything, give to the poor and follow him; the man goes away sad, and Jesus says that what is impossible for people is possible with God. He tells the twelve plainly that he will be killed and rise on the third day, but they do not understand. Near Jericho a blind man keeps calling to the Son of David and receives his sight.

In chapter 19 Zacchaeus, a rich chief tax collector, climbs a tree to see Jesus; Jesus stays at his house, and Zacchaeus gives half his possessions to the poor and repays fourfold anyone he cheated, for the Son of Man came to seek and save the lost. Jesus rides into Jerusalem on a colt as the disciples praise the king who comes in the Lord's name, weeps over the city, and drives the sellers out of the temple. In chapter 20 the leaders question his authority. His parable of tenants who kill the owner's son is aimed at them, and they know it. He answers a trap about taxes by telling them to give Caesar what is Caesar's and God what is God's, and the Sadducees' riddle about the resurrection by calling God the God of the living. In chapter 21 he praises a poor widow who gave all she had, foretells the temple's fall, persecution and the Son of Man coming in a cloud, and urges them to stay alert and pray.

In chapter 22 Judas agrees to betray him. At the Passover meal Jesus gives the bread as his body and the cup as the new covenant in his blood, and when the disciples argue over who is greatest, he says he is among them as one who serves. On the Mount of Olives he prays, "not my will but yours," and Judas arrives with a crowd and a kiss. In the high priest's courtyard Peter denies him three times, the rooster crows, the Lord looks at Peter, and Peter goes out and weeps. In chapter 23 Pilate three times finds no basis for a charge, but the crowd demands Barabbas be released and Jesus crucified, and Pilate gives in. One criminal crucified beside Jesus mocks him; the other asks to be remembered and is promised paradise that day. Darkness covers the land, the temple curtain tears, Jesus entrusts his spirit to the Father and dies, and the centurion says he was righteous. Joseph of Arimathea lays him in a tomb cut in the rock.

In chapter 24 the women find the tomb empty, and two men in gleaming clothes tell them he has been raised; the apostles call it nonsense. That day Jesus walks with two disciples to Emmaus, unrecognized, explains what the scriptures say about him, and is known when he breaks bread. He appears to the eleven, shows his hands and feet, eats fish in front of them, and sends them as witnesses that repentance and forgiveness will be proclaimed to all nations. He blesses them near Bethany and is taken up into heaven, and they return to Jerusalem with great joy.
