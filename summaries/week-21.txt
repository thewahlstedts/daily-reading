The temple is finished, and chapter 5 brings the ark, holding only the two stone tablets from Horeb, into the most holy place under the wings of the cherubim. As singers and trumpeters praise God with one voice, for he is good and his loyal love is everlasting, a cloud fills the house, and the priests cannot stand to minister because of the glory of the LORD. In chapter 6 Solomon blesses the assembly, saying God has kept his word to David, then kneels on a bronze platform with his hands spread toward heaven. Even the highest heavens cannot contain God, he admits, much less this house, yet he asks God to hear and forgive whenever people pray toward it: when Israel is defeated or suffers drought or plague because of sin, when a foreigner comes for the sake of God's name, and when captives in a far land repent and pray toward home. He ends by asking God to remember the loyal love of his servant David.

In chapter 7 fire comes down from heaven on the offerings, the glory of the LORD fills the house again, and the people bow to the pavement and give thanks. After the dedication and the feast, God appears to Solomon at night with an answer: when he sends drought, locusts or disease, if his people who are called by his name humble themselves, pray, seek his face and turn from their evil ways, he will hear from heaven, forgive their sin and heal their land. Then he sets out a choice: walk as David did and the throne will stand, but turn to other gods and Israel will be uprooted and this house made a byword. Chapter 8 lists Solomon's building projects and his forced labor drawn from the peoples left in the land, not from Israelites. He moves Pharaoh's daughter out of David's city, saying places where the ark has been are holy, keeps the offerings Moses commanded, and arranges the priests and Levites as David ordered.

Chapter 9 brings the queen of Sheba with hard questions. Solomon answers every one, and she is left breathless; she blesses God for loving Israel and making Solomon king to do justice and righteousness. The rest of the chapter is wealth: 666 talents of gold a year, an ivory throne with lions on its steps, and kings from all the earth seeking his wisdom. Solomon reigns forty years and dies. Chronicles does not recount the foreign wives and idols of 1 Kings 11 (week 7); it moves straight to chapter 10. At Shechem the people, with Jeroboam back from Egypt, ask Rehoboam to lighten the heavy yoke his father laid on them. He rejects the elders' advice for that of the young men he grew up with: his father used whips, he will use scorpions. The text calls this a turn of events from God, fulfilling Ahijah's word to Jeroboam. Israel walks away from David's house and stones Rehoboam's labor chief, and Rehoboam flees to Jerusalem.

Psalm 94 calls on God, the judge of the earth, against the proud who crush his people, kill the widow and the stranger, and say God does not see. Surely the one who planted the ear hears. God will not abandon his people, and when the psalmist's foot was slipping, God's loyal love held him up. Coming right after a king who promised a heavier yoke, the psalm can be read as an appeal against harsh rulers, though that pairing is an interpretation.
