Chapter 18 finds Jehoshaphat, rich and honored, allied by marriage to Ahab king of Israel, who asks him to join an attack on Ramoth-gilead. Jehoshaphat agrees but asks first for the word of the LORD. Four hundred prophets promise victory, but Jehoshaphat asks for another, and Ahab names Micaiah, whom he hates because he never prophesies anything good about him. Pressed to tell the truth, Micaiah sees Israel scattered like sheep without a shepherd and describes a vision of God on his throne permitting a lying spirit to speak through Ahab's prophets. He is struck and sent to prison until Ahab returns in peace, which Micaiah says will not happen. In the battle Ahab disguises himself while Jehoshaphat wears his own garments; when the chariot commanders chase him, Jehoshaphat cries out and God draws them away, but an arrow shot at random strikes Ahab, and he dies at sunset.

In chapter 19 the seer Jehu son of Hanani rebukes Jehoshaphat for helping the wicked and loving those who hate the LORD. Jehoshaphat goes out among the people to bring them back to God and appoints judges, warning them that they judge for the LORD, with whom there is no partiality or bribery. In chapter 20 a great army of Moabites and Ammonites comes against him. Afraid, he proclaims a fast, and Judah gathers at the temple. He prays to the God who rules all kingdoms and admits that they do not know what to do, but their eyes are on him. Through the Levite Jahaziel, God answers that the battle is not theirs but God's: they are to stand and see his deliverance. The next morning Jehoshaphat puts singers at the front, praising God because his loyal love is everlasting, and as they sing the enemy armies turn on one another. Judah blesses God in the valley of Berakah and returns to Jerusalem with joy. Yet the chapter closes with another alliance with a wicked king of Israel, and their ships are wrecked.

The next kings undo his work. In chapter 21 Jehoram, married to a daughter of Ahab, kills all his brothers and leads Judah astray; God spares David's house only because of his covenant and his promise of a lamp to David. Edom revolts, a letter from Elijah announces judgment, raiders carry off Jehoram's family, and he dies of a painful disease with no one's regret. In chapter 22 his son Ahaziah follows the counsel of his mother Athaliah and the house of Ahab and dies when Jehu destroys Ahab's house. Athaliah then murders the royal family and takes the throne, but the king's daughter Jehosheba, wife of the priest Jehoiada, hides Ahaziah's son Joash with his nurse in the house of God for six years. In chapter 23 Jehoiada gathers the commanders and Levites, crowns Joash in the temple, and has Athaliah put to death outside it. The people make a covenant to be the LORD's people, tear down the house of Baal, and the city is quiet.

Psalm 96 calls all the earth to sing a new song, because the gods of the peoples are idols while the LORD made the heavens; he is king and is coming to judge the world with righteousness. Much of it also appears in David's song of thanks in 1 Chronicles 16:23-33 (week 17). After a week that ends with Baal's house torn down and a son of David back on the throne, the psalm fits well, though the pairing is an interpretation.
