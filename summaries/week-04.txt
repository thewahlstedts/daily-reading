In chapter 12, God sends Nathan to David with a story about a rich man who took a poor man's only ewe lamb to feed a guest. David burns with anger and says the man deserves to die, and Nathan answers, "You are the man." God had given David Saul's house and the kingdom, yet he struck down Uriah and took his wife, so the sword will not leave his house, and his wives will be given to another in broad daylight. David says he has sinned against God; Nathan tells him God has forgiven his sin and he will not die, but the child will. David fasts and lies on the ground until the child dies, then gets up, worships and eats, saying he cannot bring him back. Bathsheba then bears Solomon, whom God loves, and Joab and David take Rabbah.

In chapter 13, David's son Amnon, on the advice of his cousin Jonadab, pretends to be ill, has Tamar sent to cook for him, and rapes her, then hates her and throws her out. Tamar tears her robe and lives desolate in the house of her brother Absalom. David is very angry. Two years later Absalom has Amnon killed at a sheep-shearing feast and flees to Geshur for three years. In chapter 14, Joab sends a wise woman from Tekoa to tell David a made-up story about two sons, one of whom killed the other, so that David will rule in her case and then see his own. David sees Joab's hand in it but lets Absalom return, though not to see his face. After two years, Absalom sets Joab's barley field on fire to get his attention, and at last the king receives and kisses him.

In chapter 15, Absalom stands at the city gate telling people with lawsuits that the king will not hear them, and so steals the hearts of Israel. After four years he goes to Hebron and has himself proclaimed king, and David's counselor Ahithophel joins him. David flees Jerusalem, leaving ten concubines behind, and sends the ark back with Zadok and Abiathar, saying God may bring him back or may do what seems good to him. He climbs the Mount of Olives weeping and barefoot, prays that God will frustrate Ahithophel's counsel, and sends his friend Hushai back to pose as Absalom's servant. In chapter 16, Ziba claims his master Mephibosheth hopes to regain Saul's kingdom, and David gives Ziba everything that was Mephibosheth's. Shimei of Saul's family curses David and throws stones, but David stops Abishai from killing him, saying God may have told him to curse. On Ahithophel's advice, Absalom goes in to his father's concubines in a tent on the roof in the sight of all Israel, matching what Nathan announced in 12:11-12.

In chapter 17, Ahithophel proposes to strike David at once with twelve thousand men. Hushai persuades Absalom to wait and gather all Israel, and the text says God had ordained this to defeat Ahithophel's good counsel and bring disaster on Absalom. Warned by messengers who hide in a well to escape Absalom's men, David crosses the Jordan to Mahanaim, where friends bring food. Ahithophel goes home, sets his house in order and hangs himself. In Psalm 73, a song of Asaph, the psalmist nearly stumbles from envy at the ease of the wicked, until he enters God's sanctuary and understands their end, and he closes by saying God holds his right hand and is the strength of his heart. Placed after a week of schemes that seem to succeed, it invites reading the two together, though that link is the plan's, not the text's.
