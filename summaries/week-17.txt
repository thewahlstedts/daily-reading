David's reign takes shape this week. In chapter 14, Hiram king of Tyre sends cedar and workmen to build David a house, and David understands that God has established him as king for the sake of his people Israel. When the Philistines attack, David asks God before each battle; the second time God tells him to circle around and wait for the sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees, for God will go out ahead of him. David's fame spreads through all the lands.

Chapter 15 is the second attempt to bring up the ark. This time David says only the Levites may carry it, because God chose them for it, and he tells them that God burst out against them the first time because they did not handle it as the law required. The Levites sanctify themselves and carry the ark on poles on their shoulders, as Moses commanded, with singers and musicians playing before it. David, in a linen ephod, leaps and dances as the ark enters the city, and Michal, Saul's daughter, despises him in her heart. In chapter 16, the ark is set in the tent David pitched, he blesses the people and gives everyone bread, dates and raisins, and he appoints Asaph and his brothers to give thanks before the ark. Their song calls Israel to remember God's covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, tells all the earth that the gods of the nations are idols while God made the heavens, and ends with thanks, for his loyal love is everlasting, and a prayer to be gathered and rescued from the nations.

In chapter 17, settled in a house of cedar, David wants to build a house for the ark, and Nathan at first agrees. That night God sends word that David is not the one to build it: God has moved from tent to tent and never asked for a house of cedar. Instead God will build David a house. He took David from following the sheep, and he will raise up one of David's sons, who will build God's house; God will be a father to him, will not take his loyal love away as he did from the one before David, and will establish his throne forever. David sits before God and asks who he is that God has brought him this far, and prays that the promise to his house be established forever. Chapters 18 and 19 tell David's victories over the Philistines, Moab, Hadadezer of Zobah, Aram and Edom; the text twice says that God gave David victory wherever he went, and David rules with justice and righteousness. When the new Ammonite king humiliates the envoys David sent to comfort him, the Ammonites hire Aramean chariots, and Joab tells his brother Abishai to be strong for their people and the cities of their God and leave the outcome to God. Aram and Ammon both flee, and Aram makes peace with David.

Psalm 89, a maskil of Ethan the Ezrahite, sings of God's loyal love and retells the covenant with David: his offspring will last forever and his throne like the sun, and even if his sons forsake God's law and are punished, God will not take back his loyal love. Then it turns sharply, saying God has rejected his anointed and thrown his crown in the dirt, and asks how long and where that sworn love has gone. Read after chapter 17 and the fall of Jerusalem in 2 Kings 25 (week 13), the psalm holds the promise and the loss side by side, though the pairing is an interpretation.
