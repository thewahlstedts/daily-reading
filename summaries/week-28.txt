Ezra 8 is told in Ezra's own voice. Camped by the river Ahava with the families traveling from Babylonia, he finds no Levites and sends for some, and Levites and temple servants join them. Ezra proclaims a fast there to seek a safe journey, because he was ashamed to ask the king for soldiers after telling him that God's hand is good to all who seek him; they fast and pray, and God answers. He entrusts the silver, gold and vessels to twelve leading priests, and God protects them from enemies and ambushes on the way to Jerusalem.

Chapter 9 turns to grief. Officials report that the people, including priests, Levites and leaders, have married women from the surrounding peoples whose detestable practices God had warned against. Ezra tears his clothes, pulls hair from his head and beard, and sits appalled until the evening offering. Then he kneels and confesses that their guilt has grown up to the heavens, that their sins led to the sword and captivity, and that God has still left them a remnant and shown them loyal love before the kings of Persia. He ends with no request, only that they stand before a righteous God in their guilt. In chapter 10 a great crowd gathers, weeping. Shecaniah admits the people have broken faith but says there is still hope, and urges a covenant to send away the foreign wives and their children. The men of Judah and Benjamin assemble in the rain in the ninth month, agree, and ask for the cases to be handled city by city. Over the following months the cases are examined, and the book ends with a list of the men involved, noting that some of the wives had borne children.

Nehemiah 1 moves to Susa, where Nehemiah is cupbearer to the king. His brother Hanani reports that the survivors in Judah are in disgrace and Jerusalem's wall is broken down and its gates burned. Nehemiah weeps, fasts and prays, confessing Israel's sins and reminding God of his word to Moses that he would gather a scattered people who return to him. In chapter 2 the king, Artaxerxes, notices his sad face. Afraid, Nehemiah prays to God and asks to be sent to rebuild the city; the king agrees and gives him letters, timber and an escort. Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite are displeased. Nehemiah inspects the walls by night, then calls the people to build, and they answer, "Let us arise and build." Chapter 3 lists who repaired each gate and section, starting with Eliashib the high priest at the Sheep Gate; Shallum works with his daughters, while the nobles of Tekoa do not take part. In chapter 4 Sanballat mocks the work, and Tobiah jokes that a fox would knock the wall down. When enemies plot an attack, Nehemiah prays, posts guards, and tells the people to remember the Lord and fight for their families. From then on, half of them work while half hold weapons, and builders keep a sword at their side.

Psalm 104 praises God as maker and keeper of everything: he set the waters in their bounds, sends springs for the animals, grows food for people, and gives all creatures their food in due season; when he sends his Spirit, they are created.
