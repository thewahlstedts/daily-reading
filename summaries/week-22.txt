In chapter 11 Rehoboam gathers an army to win back the north, but the prophet Shemaiah tells him not to fight his brothers, because this has come from God, and they obey. When Jeroboam rejects the Levites as priests and appoints his own for the high places, the goat idols and the calves he made, the Levites leave their land for Judah, followed by people from every tribe who set their hearts to seek God, and for three years they walk in the way of David and Solomon. But in chapter 12, once Rehoboam is strong, he and all Israel abandon God's law. Shishak king of Egypt takes Judah's fortified cities and comes to Jerusalem. Shemaiah announces that because they abandoned God, God has abandoned them to Shishak. The king and the princes humble themselves and say the LORD is righteous, so God spares the city, though they become Shishak's servants. Shishak takes the temple and palace treasures, including Solomon's gold shields, and Rehoboam replaces them with bronze. The verdict on him is that he did evil because he did not set his heart to seek God.

In chapter 13 his son Abijah reigns. Facing Jeroboam's larger army, Abijah stands on a mountain and reminds Israel that God gave the kingdom to David's sons by a covenant of salt, that they have golden calves and priests of what is not a god, while Judah has kept God's priests and offerings. Caught in an ambush, Judah cries out to God, the priests blow the trumpets, and God defeats Israel, because Judah relied on him. In chapter 14 Asa does what is good, tears down foreign altars and Asherah poles, and tells Judah to seek God, and the land has rest. When Zerah the Cushite comes with a vast army, Asa cries out that they rely on God and have come in his name against this multitude, and the Cushites flee. In chapter 15 the prophet Azariah tells him that God is with them while they are with him: if they seek him, he will be found, but if they forsake him, he will forsake them, much as David told Solomon in 1 Chronicles 28:9 (week 19). Asa removes idols, even deposing his mother Maacah for her Asherah image, and the people swear with all their heart to seek God, who gives them rest.

Chapters 16–17 show the same king slipping. When Baasha of Israel builds Ramah against him, Asa pays the king of Aram with temple silver and gold to attack Israel. Hanani the seer rebukes him: God gave him the Cushites when he relied on God, for God's eyes roam the earth to strengthen those whose hearts are fully his, but now he will have wars. Asa puts the seer in prison, and later, badly ill in his feet, he seeks only healers, not God. His son Jehoshaphat seeks the God of his ancestors, removes high places and sends officials, Levites and priests through Judah's towns to teach from the scroll of the law.

Psalm 95 calls worshipers to sing, bow down and kneel before the great king, their maker, whose sheep they are, and warns them not to harden their hearts as their ancestors did at Meribah and Massah in the wilderness (Exodus 17:7). After a week of kings who seek God and then drift, one way to read the pairing is as a call to keep listening today.
