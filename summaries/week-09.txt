In chapter 19, Jezebel vows to kill Elijah within a day, and he flees past Beersheba into the wilderness, sits under a broom tree and asks God to take his life. An angel twice wakes him to eat, and in that food's strength he travels forty days and nights to Horeb. Asked twice what he is doing there, Elijah answers that Israel has forsaken the covenant and killed the prophets, and he alone is left. A great wind, an earthquake and a fire pass by, but God is not in them; after them comes the sound of a gentle whisper. God sends him back to anoint Hazael over Aram, Jehu over Israel and Elisha as prophet in his place, and says seven thousand in Israel have not bowed to Baal. Elijah throws his cloak over Elisha as he plows, and Elisha leaves his oxen to follow him.

In chapter 20, Ben-Hadad of Aram besieges Samaria. A prophet tells Ahab that God will give him the victory so that he will know that he is the LORD, and a small force routs the Arameans. When Aram returns to fight on the plain, saying Israel's God is a god of the hills, Israel wins again. But Ahab spares Ben-Hadad, calls him his brother and makes a treaty with him, and a prophet in disguise tells him that his life will go for the life of the man God had devoted to destruction. In chapter 21, Naboth refuses to sell Ahab the vineyard that is his ancestors' inheritance. Jezebel writes letters in Ahab's name, has two scoundrels accuse Naboth of cursing God and the king, and Naboth is stoned. As Ahab goes to take the vineyard, Elijah meets him with God's word: the dogs will lick his blood, his house will end like Jeroboam's, and dogs will eat Jezebel. Ahab tears his clothes and humbles himself, and God delays the disaster until his son's days.

In chapter 22, Ahab and Jehoshaphat of Judah plan to retake Ramoth-Gilead. About four hundred prophets promise victory, but Jehoshaphat asks for a prophet of the LORD, and Micaiah is brought. He first echoes the others, then describes Israel scattered like sheep without a shepherd, and a vision of God sending a false spirit into the prophets' mouths. Ahab jails him and goes into battle in disguise, but an arrow strikes him between the plates of his armor, he dies at evening, and the dogs lick his blood, according to God's word. Ahab's son Ahaziah succeeds him and serves Baal.

In 2 Kings 1, Ahaziah falls through a lattice and sends to ask Baal-Zebub of Ekron whether he will recover. Elijah meets the messengers and asks whether there is no God in Israel. Fire from heaven consumes two companies of fifty sent to bring him; when the third captain begs for mercy, Elijah goes and tells the king he will die, and he does. In chapter 2, Elisha refuses three times to leave Elijah, they cross the Jordan when Elijah strikes it with his cloak, and Elisha asks for a double portion of his spirit. A chariot and horses of fire separate them, Elijah goes up in the storm, and Elisha picks up the cloak and parts the Jordan himself. He purifies Jericho's water, and some boys who mock him are mauled by two bears after he curses them. Psalm 78, a maskil of Asaph, retells Israel's history of rebellion met by both anger and compassion, so the next generation will not forget God's wonders, ending with God's choice of David. After a week of kings who forget, it can be read as a call to remember, though that pairing is the plan's.
