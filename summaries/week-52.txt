The plan's final week stays in Isaiah. In chapter 8 Isaiah's son is named Maher-Halal-Hash-Baz as a sign that before the boy can say "my father" or "my mother," the wealth of Damascus and Samaria will be carried off before the king of Assyria. Because the people refused the gently flowing waters of Shiloah, the king of Assyria will sweep over Judah like a flooding river, up to the neck. Isaiah is told not to fear what the people fear but to regard the LORD of hosts as holy; he will be a sanctuary, and also a stone to stumble over. Chapter 9 turns from that darkness: the land of Zebulun and Naphtali, Galilee of the nations, will be honored, and the people who walked in darkness have seen a great light. A child is born, a son given, with dominion on his shoulder, called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace, whose peace will have no end on David's throne. The rest of the chapter returns to judgment on proud Israel: God's anger has not turned away, and his hand is still stretched out.

Chapter 10 pronounces woe on those who write unjust decrees to rob the poor and the widow. Assyria is the rod of God's anger, sent against a godless nation, but its king thinks he conquered by his own strength, and God asks whether an ax can boast against the one who swings it. Assyria will be cut down like a forest, and a remnant of Jacob will return to the mighty God. Chapter 11 begins with a shoot from the stump of Jesse, on whom the spirit of the LORD rests, who will judge the poor with righteousness. The wolf will live with the lamb, a little child will lead them, and the earth will be full of the knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea. The LORD will gather his scattered people from the four corners of the earth, and chapter 12 is a song of thanks to God, who has become salvation, and who can be trusted without fear.

In their setting these chapters speak to Judah under threat from Aram, Israel and Assyria, and promise a coming king from David's line. The New Testament applies some of them to Jesus: Matthew 4:13-16 says his move to Capernaum, in Zebulun and Naphtali, fulfilled the words about the great light. Christians have also read the child of chapter 9 and the shoot of Jesse in chapter 11 as pointing to Jesus, a reading that comes from outside these chapters.

Chapter 13 is an oracle against Babylon: the LORD musters an army, the day of the LORD comes with darkened sun and moon, and God stirs up the Medes, so that Babylon will be overthrown like Sodom and Gomorrah and left to wild animals. In chapter 14 God has compassion on Jacob and gives his people rest, and they raise a taunt over the king of Babylon, the fallen morning star who said he would ascend to heaven and make himself like the Most High, but is brought down to Sheol. The chapter ends with God's sworn plan to break Assyria and a warning to Philistia. Psalms 133–135 close the plan with how good it is when brothers live together in unity, a call to bless the LORD by night in his house, and praise for the God who chose Jacob, struck the firstborn of Egypt, and is greater than idols that have mouths but cannot speak.
