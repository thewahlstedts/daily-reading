The exile is over. Ezra 1 opens in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, when God stirs the king's spirit to fulfill the word spoken through Jeremiah. Cyrus announces that God has appointed him to build a house in Jerusalem and invites God's people to go up and build it. He also returns the temple vessels Nebuchadnezzar had taken, counting them out to Sheshbazzar the prince of Judah. Chapter 2 lists those who returned with Zerubbabel, Jeshua and others, family by family and town by town. Some priestly families cannot find their names in the genealogy records and are kept from the holy food until a priest can consult the Urim and Thummim. The whole assembly numbers 42,360, besides servants.

In chapter 3 the people gather in Jerusalem in the seventh month. Jeshua and Zerubbabel build the altar first, though they are afraid of the peoples of the lands, and keep the feast of booths. In the second year the temple's foundation is laid; the priests and Levites sing that God is good and his loyal love lasts forever, and the people shout. Many older priests, Levites and family heads who had seen the first temple weep aloud, and no one can tell the weeping from the shouts of joy. Chapter 4 brings opposition. The enemies of Judah and Benjamin ask to help build, saying they have worshiped Israel's God since an Assyrian king brought them there; Zerubbabel and Jeshua refuse, and the people of the land discourage the builders and bribe officials against them. The chapter also records a letter to King Artaxerxes warning that if Jerusalem's walls are finished the city will stop paying tribute; the king orders the work stopped, and it is stopped by force. Work on the house of God halts until the second year of Darius.

In chapter 5 the prophets Haggai and Zechariah prophesy, and Zerubbabel and Jeshua start building again. Tattenai the governor asks who authorized them, but the eye of their God is on the elders, and the work goes on while he writes to Darius. In chapter 6 Cyrus's decree turns up in Ecbatana, and Darius orders Tattenai to stay away, pay the costs from royal taxes and supply the sacrifices. The house is finished in the sixth year of Darius and dedicated with joy, with twelve male goats offered for the twelve tribes, and the returned exiles keep the Passover. Chapter 7 introduces Ezra, a priest descended from Aaron and a scribe skilled in the law of Moses, who had set his heart to seek the law, do it and teach it. He arrives in Jerusalem in the seventh year of Artaxerxes with the king's letter giving him silver and gold and authority to appoint judges and teach the law, and he blesses God for putting this in the king's heart.

Psalm 103 calls the soul to bless God and not forget his benefits: he forgives, heals and redeems, is compassionate and slow to anger, and removes transgressions as far as east is from west. Human days are like grass, but his loyal love is from everlasting to everlasting on those who fear him. Read alongside Ezra, one way to hear it is as the song of a people given a second start, though that pairing is an interpretation, not something the text states.
