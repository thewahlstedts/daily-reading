In chapter 29, the Philistines gather at Aphek for war with Israel, and David and his men march at the rear with Achish. The other Philistine commanders, remembering the song about Saul's thousands and David's ten thousands, fear he would turn on them in battle, so Achish, who has found no fault in him, sends him back. In chapter 30, David's men reach Ziklag to find that Amalekites have burned it and carried off their wives and children, David's two wives among them. The men weep until they have no strength left and talk of stoning David, but David strengthens himself in God, asks through the ephod whether to pursue, and is told he will overtake the raiders and rescue everyone. Two hundred men are too exhausted to cross the Wadi Besor; with the other four hundred, and guided by an abandoned Egyptian servant, David defeats the Amalekites and recovers everything and everyone. When some of his men want to keep the plunder from those who stayed behind, David says God gave them the victory and that those who guard the baggage share alike, and this becomes a rule in Israel.

Chapter 31 tells of Saul's end on Mount Gilboa. The Philistines kill Jonathan and two other sons of Saul, and Saul, badly wounded by archers, falls on his own sword when his armor bearer will not kill him. The Philistines fasten his body to the wall of Beth Shan, and the men of Jabesh Gilead travel all night to take it down, burn the bodies, bury the bones and fast seven days. In 2 Samuel 1 an Amalekite brings David Saul's crown and bracelet and claims he killed the wounded king at his request, a story that differs from chapter 31. David and his men tear their clothes and mourn, and David has the man put to death for striking God's anointed. Then David sings a lament over Saul and Jonathan, with the refrain "how the mighty have fallen," and speaks to Jonathan directly: his love was more wonderful to David than the love of women.

In chapter 2, God sends David to Hebron, where the men of Judah anoint him king over Judah, and he blesses the men of Jabesh Gilead for their loyalty to Saul. Abner, Saul's army commander, makes Saul's son Ish-Bosheth king over Israel, and fighting breaks out at the pool of Gibeon. Asahel, Joab's swift brother, chases Abner and ignores two warnings to turn aside, and Abner kills him with the butt of his spear. Chapter 3 says David's house grew stronger while Saul's grew weaker. After Ish-Bosheth accuses Abner over Saul's concubine Rizpah, Abner offers to bring all Israel over to David, who first demands his wife Michal back; her husband Paltiel follows her, weeping. Abner is received in peace at Hebron, but Joab, telling David that Abner came to deceive him, brings him back without David's knowledge and kills him in the gate to avenge Asahel. David declares himself innocent, curses Joab's house, and mourns and fasts for Abner, and the people see that the king had not wanted him dead. Psalm 71 is the prayer of someone who has trusted God from youth and asks not to be abandoned in old age, while enemies say God has left him; one way to read it beside this week is as a voice of steady trust amid betrayal and bloodshed, though the plan does not say so.
