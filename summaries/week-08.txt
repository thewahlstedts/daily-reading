Chapter 13 begins at Bethel, where Jeroboam stands at his altar. A man of God from Judah cries out against it, announcing that a son of David's house named Josiah will one day sacrifice its priests on it. When Jeroboam orders him seized, the king's outstretched hand is paralyzed and the altar splits; at the man's prayer the hand is restored. The man of God refuses the king's hospitality, since God has told him not to eat or drink there. But an old prophet of Bethel lies to him, claiming an angel told him to bring him home for a meal. He goes back and eats, and on the road a lion kills him, then stands beside the body without eating it. The old prophet buries him in his own tomb, saying his word against the altar will surely come true, and Jeroboam still does not turn from his evil way.

In chapter 14, Jeroboam's son Abijah falls ill, and Jeroboam sends his wife in disguise to the blind prophet Ahijah, who knows her by her footsteps because God has told him. Because Jeroboam made other gods and idols, disaster will sweep away his house, and Israel will be uprooted and scattered beyond the River. The boy dies as she reaches home. In Judah, under Rehoboam, the people build high places and sacred poles, and Shishak king of Egypt carries off the temple treasures and Solomon's gold shields. In chapter 15, Abijam of Judah follows his father's sins, though God keeps a lamp in Jerusalem for David's sake. Asa does right, removes idols and removes Maacah as queen mother, though not the high places, and pays the king of Aram to draw off Baasha. Baasha of Israel kills Jeroboam's son Nadab and wipes out Jeroboam's house, as Ahijah had said.

In chapter 16, the prophet Jehu son of Hanani says Baasha's house will end like Jeroboam's, and it does: Zimri kills Baasha's son Elah while he is drunk and destroys the family, then rules seven days before burning the palace over himself when the army's choice, Omri, besieges Tirzah. Omri builds Samaria and does more evil than all before him, and his son Ahab goes further, marrying Jezebel of Sidon and serving Baal. The chapter closes with Hiel rebuilding Jericho at the cost of two sons, as Joshua had said (Joshua 6:26).

Then Elijah appears. In chapter 17 he tells Ahab there will be no dew or rain except at his word. God feeds him by crows at the Wadi Kerith, then sends him to a widow at Zarephath whose last handful of flour and little oil do not run out. When her son dies, Elijah stretches himself over the boy three times and prays, and God gives the child life again. In chapter 18, in the third year, Elijah meets Ahab, and on Mount Carmel he asks the people how long they will limp between two opinions. The prophets of Baal call on Baal from morning to evening with no answer, while Elijah rebuilds God's altar with twelve stones, has it drenched, and prays; fire falls and consumes everything, and the people cry that the LORD is God. The prophets of Baal are killed at the Kishon, and after Elijah sends his servant to look toward the sea seven times, heavy rain comes. Psalm 77 asks in a sleepless night whether God's loyal love has ended, then remembers his wonders, how the waters saw him and trembled. Beside Elijah's lonely stand it can be read as a prayer for God to act again, though that pairing is an interpretation.
