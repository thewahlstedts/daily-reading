In chapter 15 men from Judea teach that Gentile believers must be circumcised to be saved, and Paul and Barnabas go up to the apostles and elders in Jerusalem. Peter says God gave the Gentiles the Holy Spirit just as he did to the Jewish believers, cleansing their hearts by faith, so why lay on them a yoke no one has been able to bear. James cites the prophets on rebuilding the fallen tent of David so that the Gentiles may seek the Lord, and a letter asks Gentile believers only to keep away from food sacrificed to idols, blood, what has been strangled and sexual immorality. Then Paul and Barnabas part after a sharp disagreement over John Mark; Barnabas sails to Cyprus, and Paul takes Silas.

In chapter 16 Timothy, whose father is Greek, joins Paul, who circumcises him because of the Jews in those places, and at Troas a vision of a Macedonian man calls them over; from here the narrative says "we." In Philippi, Lydia, a seller of purple cloth, is baptized with her household. When Paul drives a spirit of divination out of a slave girl, her owners drag Paul and Silas before the magistrates, who have them beaten and jailed. At midnight, as they pray and sing, an earthquake opens the doors, and the jailer, stopped by Paul from killing himself, is baptized with his household. Chapter 17 moves through Thessalonica, where opponents charge that these men say there is another king, Jesus, and Berea, where people examine the scriptures daily. In Athens, starting from an altar to an unknown God, Paul tells the Areopagus that the maker of the world does not live in temples made by hands, is not far from anyone, and now commands all people to repent, having given proof by raising a man from the dead. Some scoff; a few believe.

In chapter 18 Paul works in Corinth with Aquila and Priscilla, tentmakers who had left Rome under Claudius's order. After the Lord tells him in a night vision not to be afraid, because many people in the city are his, he stays a year and six months, and the proconsul Gallio refuses to judge the case against him. In Ephesus, Priscilla and Aquila explain the way of God more accurately to Apollos. In chapter 19 Paul finds about twelve disciples there who knew only John's baptism; they are baptized in Jesus' name and receive the Spirit. He teaches for two years, the seven sons of Sceva are overpowered when they try to use Jesus' name, and former practitioners of magic burn their books. Demetrius the silversmith, fearing for his trade and for Artemis, stirs up a riot in the theater.

In chapter 20 Eutychus falls from a third-story window during Paul's long message and is picked up dead, but Paul embraces him and he is taken away alive, and at Miletus Paul says farewell to the Ephesian elders: he is going to Jerusalem bound by the Spirit, expecting bonds and persecutions, and they must shepherd the church of God against the wolves to come, recalling the Lord Jesus' saying that it is more blessed to give than to receive. In chapter 21 the prophet Agabus binds himself with Paul's belt to show what awaits him, and Paul says he is ready to die in Jerusalem. There, to answer rumors that he teaches Jews to abandon Moses, he joins four men in purification at the temple, but Jews from Asia accuse him of bringing Greeks inside. A Roman tribune pulls him from the mob, and the week ends with Paul on the barracks steps, about to address the crowd in Aramaic.
