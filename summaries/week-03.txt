In chapter 4, two of Ish-Bosheth's raiding captains, Recab and Baanah, kill him as he rests at noon and bring his head to David at Hebron. David reminds them that he executed the man who claimed to have killed Saul, and has them put to death. The chapter also introduces Jonathan's son Mephibosheth, lamed at five when he fell as his nurse fled with him. In chapter 5, all the tribes come to Hebron, and the elders anoint David king over Israel. He captures the fortress of Zion from the Jebusites and calls it the city of David, Hiram king of Tyre sends cedar and craftsmen to build him a house, and David recognizes that God has established his kingdom for the sake of his people. Twice he asks God before facing the Philistines, and twice he wins.

Chapter 6 brings the ark toward Jerusalem on a new cart, with David and all Israel dancing before God. When the oxen stumble, Uzza takes hold of the ark and God strikes him dead. David, angry and afraid, leaves the ark with Obed-Edom, whose household God blesses for three months, then brings it up with joy, dancing with all his might in a linen ephod. Michal, Saul's daughter, despises him and mocks him, and David answers that he celebrated before the God who chose him over her father; she has no child to the day of her death. In chapter 7, David wants to build a house for the ark, and Nathan at first agrees, but that night God tells Nathan he has never asked for a cedar house. Instead God will build David a house: he will raise up David's offspring, who will build a house for God's name, and will establish his throne forever. God will be a father to him and discipline him when he does wrong, but will not take his loyal love away as he did from Saul. David sits before God and prays in wonder, asking who he is that God has brought him this far.

Chapter 8 summarizes David's victories over the Philistines, Moab, Hadadezer of Zobah, Aram and Edom, and says God protected him wherever he went. In chapter 9, David looks for anyone left of Saul's house to whom he can show loyal love for Jonathan's sake. Mephibosheth is given back Saul's land and eats at the king's table like one of his sons. In chapter 10, David's kindness to the new Ammonite king Hanun is taken for spying; his envoys are shamed, and the Ammonites hire Aramean armies. Joab routs the Arameans while Abishai drives the Ammonites back into their city, and then David himself defeats the regrouped Arameans.

In chapter 11, while the army besieges Rabbah, David stays in Jerusalem, sees Bathsheba bathing, learns she is the wife of Uriah the Hittite, and sleeps with her. When she tells him she is pregnant, David calls Uriah home, but Uriah will not go to his house while the ark and the army are camped in the field, even after David gets him drunk. David sends him back to Joab with a letter ordering him placed where the fighting is fiercest and abandoned there, and Uriah dies. David marries Bathsheba, and the chapter ends by saying that what David had done was evil in God's eyes. Psalm 72, titled "Of Solomon," prays for a king who judges with righteousness, defends the poor and needy, and counts their blood precious. One way to read it after chapter 11 is as the measure of kingship David has just failed, though the plan leaves that connection to the reader.
