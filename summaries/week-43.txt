Chapter 8 opens with Saul approving of Stephen's death and a great persecution that scatters the believers through Judea and Samaria, while Saul drags men and women off to prison. Philip proclaims the Christ in Samaria, and even Simon, a magician the people called the great power of God, believes and is baptized. When Peter and John come and the Samaritans receive the Spirit through the laying on of hands, Simon offers money for that power, and Peter tells him to repent, since God's gift cannot be bought. Then an angel sends Philip to the desert road toward Gaza, where an Ethiopian official of Queen Candace is reading Isaiah about one led like a sheep to the slaughter. He asks whom the prophet means; Philip tells him about Jesus, baptizes him at some water by the road, and the man goes on rejoicing. One way to read these chapters is as the widening Jesus spoke of in 1:8.

In chapter 9 Saul heads to Damascus with letters to arrest followers of the Way. A light from heaven flashes around him and a voice asks why he is persecuting him: it is Jesus. Blind for three days, Saul is visited by Ananias, who is reluctant until the Lord calls Saul his chosen instrument to carry his name to Gentiles, kings and Israel. Something like scales falls from his eyes, he is baptized, and he begins proclaiming Jesus as the Son of God; when a plot follows, he escapes over the wall in a basket. Peter heals Aeneas, paralyzed for eight years, in Lydda, and in Joppa raises Tabitha, a disciple known for her good deeds.

In chapter 10 an angel tells Cornelius, a God-fearing centurion in Caesarea, to send for Peter. On a rooftop in Joppa, Peter sees a sheet let down from heaven full of animals and hears a voice tell him to kill and eat; when he refuses anything unclean, the voice says not to call unclean what God has made clean, three times over. Peter goes with Cornelius's men and tells the household that God has shown him not to call anyone unclean and that God shows no partiality. While he is still speaking about Jesus, the Holy Spirit falls on the Gentile listeners, and Peter has them baptized. In chapter 11 he explains this to critics in Jerusalem, asking who he was to hinder God, and they praise God that he has granted repentance to the Gentiles too. In Antioch many believe, Hellenists among them; Barnabas brings Saul there, the disciples are first called Christians, and they send famine relief to Judea. In chapter 12 Herod kills James the brother of John and jails Peter, but while the church prays an angel leads him out past the guards. When people hail Herod's voice as a god's, he is struck down for not giving glory to God, while the word of God keeps spreading.

In chapter 13 the Spirit tells the church in Antioch to set apart Barnabas and Saul, and they sail to Cyprus, where Saul, also called Paul, tells the magician Elymas, who opposes them, that he will be blind; mist falls over him, and the proconsul believes. In Pisidian Antioch Paul preaches in the synagogue, tracing Israel's story from Egypt to David and on to the risen Jesus. When some Jews oppose him, he and Barnabas turn to the Gentiles, quoting the call to be a light for the Gentiles, and are driven out. In chapter 14, after threats in Iconium, at Lystra Paul heals a man lame from birth, the crowd hails Barnabas as Zeus and Paul as Hermes, and the two tear their clothes and point the crowd to the living God who made heaven and earth. Then Paul is stoned and left for dead, yet he gets up; after Derbe they retrace their steps appointing elders and report in Antioch that God has opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.
