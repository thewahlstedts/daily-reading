The plan turns to Acts, which opens addressed to Theophilus. In chapter 1 the risen Jesus appears to the apostles over forty days and tells them to wait in Jerusalem for the Holy Spirit; then they will be his witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria and to the farthest part of the earth. He is taken up and a cloud hides him, and two men in white say he will come back the same way. About 120 believers gather to pray, and Peter says Judas's place must be filled, citing the Psalms, including the line about another taking his position (Psalm 109:8, from week 35). They pray and cast lots, and the lot falls on Matthias.

Chapter 2 is Pentecost. A sound like a rushing wind fills the house, tongues like fire rest on each of them, and they are filled with the Spirit and speak in other languages, so that Jews from many nations hear the great deeds of God in their own tongues. Some say they are drunk. Peter answers that it is only the third hour of the day and that this is what Joel foretold, the Spirit poured out on sons and daughters, young and old. God raised the Jesus they crucified, he says, and he quotes Psalm 110:1 (week 36) about sitting at God's right hand. Cut to the heart, the crowd asks what to do; Peter tells them to repent and be baptized, and about three thousand are added. The believers devote themselves to the apostles' teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread and prayer, and share with anyone in need.

In chapter 3 Peter tells a man lame from birth at the Beautiful Gate that he has no silver or gold, but in the name of Jesus he should walk, and the man goes into the temple walking, leaping and praising God. Peter tells the crowd it was faith in Jesus' name, the one they denied before Pilate and God raised, and calls them to repent. In chapter 4 Peter and John are arrested, though the believing men now number about five thousand. Before the council Peter says the man was healed by the name of Jesus, the stone the builders rejected (Psalm 118:22, from week 40), and that there is salvation in no other name. Ordered to stop, they answer that they cannot stop speaking of what they have seen and heard. The believers pray for boldness, the place is shaken, and they speak God's word boldly. Barnabas, a Levite from Cyprus, sells a field and gives the money to the apostles.

Chapter 5 brings a contrast: Ananias and Sapphira keep back part of the money from land they sold, Peter says they have lied to the Holy Spirit, and each falls dead. The apostles are jailed, freed at night by an angel, and found teaching in the temple again. They tell the council they must obey God rather than people, and the Pharisee Gamaliel advises leaving them alone, since if this is from God it cannot be stopped. They are beaten and leave rejoicing to be counted worthy of dishonor for the name. In chapter 6, when the Greek-speaking believers' widows are overlooked in the daily food, seven men are chosen to take charge, among them Stephen and Philip. False witnesses accuse Stephen of speaking against the temple and the law. In chapter 7 he answers with Israel's history, from Abraham and Joseph to Moses, whom the people rejected, and says the Most High does not live in houses made by hands. He calls his hearers stiff-necked, like their fathers who killed the prophets. He sees Jesus standing at God's right hand; they stone him, laying their cloaks at the feet of a young man named Saul, and he prays that this sin not be held against them.
