Chapter 28 gathers Israel's leaders in Jerusalem for David's last charge. He had wanted to build a house of rest for the ark, but God refused because he was a man of war who had shed blood. Instead God chose Solomon, out of David's many sons, to sit on the throne of the kingdom of the LORD, promising to establish his kingdom forever if he keeps God's commandments. David urges Solomon to serve God with a whole heart and a willing mind: if he seeks God, he will find him, but if he abandons him, God will reject him forever. He hands over written plans for the temple that he says came from God, and tells Solomon to be strong and courageous, since God will not forsake him until the work is done.

In chapter 29 David gives his own gold and silver for the house and asks who else will give willingly. The leaders do, and the people rejoice because they have given with a whole heart. David blesses God before the assembly: greatness, power and the kingdom are his, everything comes from him, and what they have given came from his hand. They are strangers and sojourners like their ancestors, with days like a shadow. Solomon is made king a second time and anointed, and David dies in a good old age after reigning forty years, full of days, wealth and honor.

Second Chronicles 1 finds Solomon at the high place in Gibeon, where the tent of assembly from Moses' time still stood. He offers a thousand burnt offerings, and that night God tells him to ask for whatever he wants. Solomon asks for wisdom and knowledge to judge this great people. Because he did not ask for wealth, honor or long life, God gives him wisdom and also wealth and honor beyond any king before or after him, and by the chapter's end silver and gold are as common as stones in Jerusalem. In chapter 2 Solomon writes to Huram king of Tyre that the house will be great because their God is greater than all gods, though the highest heavens cannot contain him. Huram agrees to send timber and sends a skilled craftsman also named Huram. Chapter 3 places the temple on Mount Moriah, at the threshing floor of Ornan where God had appeared to David, with gold-covered walls, two great cherubim whose wings span the most holy place, and two columns named Jakin and Boaz. Genesis 22:2 sends Abraham to the land of Moriah to offer Isaac, a link the chapter itself does not draw. Chapter 4 adds the bronze altar, the great sea on twelve oxen, and the gold furnishings.

Psalm 91 speaks of the one who lives in the shelter of the Most High: God covers him with his feathers, he finds refuge under God's wings, and he need not fear terror by night or the arrow by day. God's angels will keep him from striking his foot against a stone, the lines the devil quoted to Jesus in Luke 4:10-11 (week 16). The psalm ends with God promising to answer the one who calls on him and to be with him in trouble. Beside a temple whose cherubim spread their wings across the most holy place, the image of refuge under God's wings can take on extra weight, though that pairing is an interpretation.
