In Luke 7 a centurion sends word that Jesus need not come under his roof but only say the word, and Jesus says he has not found such faith even in Israel. At Nain he has compassion on a widow and raises her only son. When John the Baptist asks whether he is the one who is to come, Jesus points to the blind seeing, the dead raised and the poor hearing good news. At a Pharisee's dinner a woman known as a sinner wets his feet with her tears and anoints them, and Jesus tells his host Simon a parable of two debtors: the one forgiven more loves more. He tells her that her sins are forgiven.

Chapter 8 names women who supported Jesus and the twelve, among them Mary Magdalene and Joanna. In the parable of the sower the seed is the word of God, and the good soil is those who hold it fast and bear fruit with endurance. Jesus calms a storm, sends a legion of demons out of a man among the Gerasenes into a herd of pigs, heals a woman who touched his cloak after twelve years of bleeding, and raises Jairus's daughter. In chapter 9 he sends out the twelve, feeds about five thousand men with five loaves and two fish, and Peter calls him the Christ of God. He says the Son of Man must suffer, be killed and be raised, and that his followers must take up their cross every day. On the mountain Moses and Elijah speak with him about his departure in Jerusalem, and a voice from the cloud calls him God's Son, the Chosen One, and tells the disciples to listen to him. Near the end of the chapter Jesus sets his face toward Jerusalem and answers three would-be followers, the last with the saying that no one who puts his hand on the plow and looks back is fit for the kingdom of God.

In chapter 10 he sends out seventy-two others and tells them to rejoice not that demons submit to them but that their names are written in heaven. A legal expert asks who his neighbor is, and Jesus answers with the Samaritan who cares for a beaten man whom a priest and a Levite passed by. At Martha's house he says that Mary, listening at his feet, has chosen the better part. In chapter 11 he teaches the disciples to pray to the Father and to ask, seek and knock. Accused of driving out demons by Beelzebul, he says he does it by the finger of God. He recalls the queen of the south who came to hear Solomon's wisdom (compare the queen of Sheba in 1 Kings 10, week 7) and says something greater than Solomon is here, then pronounces woes on Pharisees and legal experts.

Chapter 12 warns against hypocrisy and greed. A rich man plans bigger barns, but God tells him his life is demanded that very night. Jesus tells his disciples not to be anxious, since God feeds the ravens and clothes the lilies, but to seek his kingdom and stay ready like servants waiting for their master. Psalm 92, a song for the Sabbath, says the wicked flourish only like grass, while the righteous flourish like a palm tree, planted in God's house and still prospering in old age. Psalm 93 declares that the LORD is king, mightier than the breakers of the sea. Read after Luke 8, where the disciples ask who this is that even the wind and water obey, the psalm can sound like an answer, though that pairing is an interpretation.
