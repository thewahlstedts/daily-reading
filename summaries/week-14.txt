This week is almost all genealogy, and the lists carry short stories and notes worth catching. Chapter 2 names Israel's twelve sons and then follows Judah's family. Along the way it remembers Er, whom God put to death, Tamar as the mother of Perez and Zerah, and Achar, the troubler of Israel who was unfaithful over the devoted thing. The main line runs through Boaz to Jesse and his seventh son, David, with David's sisters Zeruiah and Abigail, the mothers of Joab, Abishai, Asahel and Amasa. Caleb's branch includes Bezalel, and Sheshan, who had only daughters, gives one to his Egyptian slave Jarha, through whom his line continues. Chapter 3 lists David's sons born in Hebron, where he reigned seven and a half years, and in Jerusalem, where he reigned thirty-three. Then it traces the kings of Judah from Solomon to Josiah and carries the line past the exile, through Jeconiah "the captive" and Zerubbabel, for several more generations.

Chapter 4 adds more of Judah's clans, including Jabez, more honored than his brothers, who asked the God of Israel to bless him, enlarge his border and keep him from harm, and God granted what he asked. Simeon's families follow; in Hezekiah's day some of them took new pasture land, and some settled at Mount Seir. Chapter 5 explains that Reuben, though firstborn, lost his birthright for defiling his father's bed; it went to Joseph's sons, though Judah grew strong and a ruler came from him. The tribes east of the Jordan, Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh, cried out to God in battle against the Hagrites and he answered them because they trusted in him. But they were unfaithful and went after the gods of the land, so God stirred up the kings of Assyria to take them into exile.

Chapter 6 is Levi. It traces the priestly line from Aaron through Zadok to Jehozadak, who went into exile when God sent Judah away by the hand of Nebuchadnezzar. It names the singers David appointed for the house where the ark rested, Heman (a grandson of Samuel, whose line goes back to Korah), Asaph and Ethan, notes that Aaron and his sons made the offerings and atonement for Israel, and lists the towns given to the Levites, including the cities of refuge. Chapter 7 covers Issachar, Benjamin, Naphtali, Manasseh, Ephraim and Asher, with counts of their fighting men. One sad note stands out: when men of Gath killed Ephraim's sons over livestock, he mourned many days and named a later son Beriah because disaster had come on his house. His line leads to Joshua son of Nun.

The psalms are both "of the sons of Korah," the ancestor named in Heman's line in chapter 6. Psalm 84 longs for God's house, where even the bird and the swallow find a home near the altars, and says one day in God's courts is better than a thousand elsewhere. Psalm 85 remembers that God once restored Jacob's fortunes and forgave, asks him to restore his people again, and looks for a day when loyal love and faithfulness meet and righteousness and peace kiss. Since these lists keep mentioning exile, the plea of Psalm 85 fits them well, though that pairing is an interpretation.
